Who We Are

Student mentors building access.

Spark Gears exists to make high-quality robotics education easier to start, easier to teach, and easier to share.

Spark Gears Initiative is a nonprofit that delivers free, hands-on robotics courses for youth ages 8 and up. We focus on expanding access by running community-based programs at no cost and maintaining an open, shareable curriculum library so educators and teams anywhere can replicate and scale our impact.

Our work is grounded in practical learning. Students gain confidence through hands-on projects that teach programming, CAD, the engineering design process, and FTC-style competitive robotics. Each course is designed to help students build, test, troubleshoot, and explain their work with the support of experienced student mentors.

Host a Program

Bring a course to your community.

If you're looking to host one of our programs, our model is simple: we provide all plans, training, and necessary funding for the course. You provide a location, your time, and students. We're also able to provide volunteer verification for teachers, and all help from Spark Gears is free of charge.

Ideal for FTC Teams

FTC Introductory Camp

A weeklong summer camp where rising 6th-9th graders build and program an FTC-scale robot as part of a mini competitive team.

This weeklong camp immerses rising 6th-9th graders in the real-world process of building an FTC-scale robot while working as part of one of two mini competitive teams. Guided step-by-step by experienced competitive robotics mentors, students get direct experience with electronics, Java programming, robot design, and hands-on troubleshooting as they prepare to take on the official FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC) DECODE Challenge. Over the week, teams design, build, and market their own robots, learn how to brand and promote a team, and solve real problems faced by actual FTC teams. The camp concludes with a game-day test and a showcase. No previous coding or building experience is needed — everyone receives detailed mentorship. It's great for FTC team outreach: volunteer hours can be verified by Spark Gears and the team can be listed as a partner with the organization, boosting competition judging.

We provide: all parts needed for every student, including Arduino boards, wires, and sensors (around $200 total), complete lesson plans for each day, and training for teachers — no prior Arduino experience needed.

Hosts provide: space, students, and marketing for the event, plus time to learn from the training and teach students.

Students provide: a personal or school-issued laptop.

Schedule: Day 1 — introductions, problem statement, and team branding. Day 2 — building/design mini-lesson, start building the robot. Day 3 — continue building. Day 4 — programming mini-lesson, program the robot. Day 5 — finishing touches and a final showcase test.

Logistics & safety: target grades 5–9, ideal group size ~5–8 students (8+ if two robots are available), ~3 hours per day, and safety glasses required for all mentors and students.

Beginner Friendly

3-Session Arduino Workshops

A hands-on, standalone mini-course for grades 6–8 that builds core engineering habits through lights, sounds, and motion.

This 3-session Arduino mini-course is designed for grades 6–8 and is hands-on and beginner-friendly. Students see immediate results — lights, sounds, and motion — while building core engineering habits like testing, iterating, and debugging. Each session is standalone, so students can attend one, two, or all three and still complete a fun project. By the end, students understand the big Arduino idea: hardware + code = interactive systems, and they've practiced a real engineering workflow: wire, upload, test, debug, improve.
