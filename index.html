Who We Are

Student mentors building access.

Spark Gears exists to make high-quality robotics education easier to start, easier to teach, and easier to share.

Spark Gears Initiative is a nonprofit that helps teams and community groups run free, hands-on robotics courses for youth ages 8 and up. We provide Arduino kits, lesson plans, slides, activities, and guides so local organizers can teach robotics without needing to create a full program from scratch.

Our work is grounded in practical learning. Students gain confidence through hands-on projects that teach programming, CAD, the engineering design process, and FTC-style competitive robotics. Each program is designed to help students build, test, troubleshoot, and explain their work with the support of student mentors.

Learn by Building

Students develop confidence through hands-on projects that turn engineering ideas into working mechanisms, code, and presentations.

Access First

We remove barriers by offering free courses and welcoming students of all backgrounds to explore robotics.

Share What Works

We publish course materials and provide teaching resources free of charge so lessons can be repeated and improved.

Goals

What we are building toward.

The initiative is structured around durable, community-run education rather than one-time demonstrations.

Accessible STEM

Bring robotics, coding, CAD, and engineering design to students who may not otherwise have access to structured technical programs.

Reusable Curriculum

Maintain Arduino kit guides, lesson plans, slides, activities, and event formats that other groups can use without starting from scratch.

Local Leadership

Help robotics teams, STEM clubs, schools, and community organizations plan logistics, market to students, and teach in their own communities.

Events

Programs in the community.

Our programs turn open teaching materials, Arduino kits, and student mentorship into real learning experiences: hackathons, robotics courses, summer camps, and public showcases.

May 2026

Code Crush Hackathon 2

Our second annual hackathon coding competition invited students to spend 24 hours creating a game or project related to a shared theme. The event also included bootcamps in marketing, AI/ML, Java, CAD, MVP pitching, and project presentation so participants could strengthen both technical and communication skills.

Spring & Summer 2026

Curie Autonomous Robotics Course

Students learned Arduino development, servo control, motor-based robot movement, sensors, and how to troubleshoot hardware and software issues. The course was built for students with no prior robotics, programming, or technical experience and used project work to make abstract engineering concepts concrete.

June 2025

Code Crush Hackathon

Our first annual hackathon coding competition gave students 24 hours to create a themed game or project. We also conducted introductory bootcamps in Python, Java, and AI so newer participants could learn core tools before building and presenting their final projects.

August 2025

Curie Summer Camp

Students created their own mini FTC team for a week. Together, they learned about FIRST, built and programmed an FTC-style robot, practiced collaboration, and showcased their final robot to an audience.

March 2025

ACL Maker Showcase

At the Academies of Loudoun Maker Showcase, we presented FIRST, competitive robotics, and our team’s engineering process to educators, parents, and students. The booth included robot demos, hardware and software explanations, outreach displays, and conversations about how students can get involved in robotics.
